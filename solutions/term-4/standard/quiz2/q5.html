Problem

A retailer increased the price of an item by 15% due to a supply shortage. Two months later, the store ran a promotion and decreased this new price by 10%.

What was the total increase (%) in the price of the item from its original value?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Set a Baseline Value

When working with successive percentage changes, it is often easiest to assume a concrete starting value. Let's assume the original price of the item is \( \$100 \).

Step 2: The Price Journey

We apply the percentage changes sequentially, making sure the second change applies to the new price, not the original \( \$100 \).

  • First, a \( 15\% \) increase on \( \$100 \) is \( +\$15 \). The new price is \( \$115 \).
  • Next, the \( 10\% \) decrease is calculated on this new price. \( 10\% \) of \( \$115 \) is \( \$11.50 \).
  • The final price is \( \$115 - \$11.50 = \) \( \$103.50 \).

Step 3: Calculate Final Percentage Change

We compare the final price directly to our baseline assumed price:

\( \$103.50 - \$100.00 = +\$3.50 \)

Since we started with exactly \( \$100 \), an increase of \( \$3.50 \) is exactly a \( 3.5\% \) increase.

3.5%
