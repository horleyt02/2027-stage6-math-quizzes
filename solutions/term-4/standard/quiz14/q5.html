Problem

The box plot below shows the pulse rates (beats/min) of \( 60 \) people after exercise at a local fitness centre.

Which of these pulse rates could be considered an outlier for the recorded data?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Read \( Q_1 \) and \( Q_3 \) from the Box Plot and Find the IQR

The left and right edges of the box give the lower quartile (\( Q_1 \)) and upper quartile (\( Q_3 \)):

\( Q_1 = 89 \)   and   \( Q_3 = 105 \)
\[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 105 - 89 = 16 \]
\[ 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 1.5 \times 16 = 24 \]

Step 2: Calculate the Lower and Upper Outlier Boundaries

A score is considered an outlier if it is strictly less than \( Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \) or strictly greater than \( Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \):

\[ \text{Lower boundary} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 89 - 24 = 65 \]
\[ \text{Upper boundary} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 105 + 24 = 129 \]
Outliers must satisfy \( x < 65 \) or \( x > 129 \)  (strict inequalities!)

Step 3: Plot All Four Options Against the Outlier Fences

Comparing each option against the non-outlier interval \( [65, 129] \) shows that \( 64 \) is the only value strictly inside an outlier zone (and within the recorded range \( [60, 135] \)):

Outlier Zones (\( < 65 \) or \( > 129 \)) Non-Outlier Zone (\( [65, 129] \))
\[ 64 < 65 \implies 64 \text{ is an outlier!} \]

Therefore, Option A is the correct choice.
