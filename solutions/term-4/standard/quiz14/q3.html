Problem

The basin in a bathroom is hemispherical in shape and has a diameter of \( 50\text{ cm} \).

Given that \( 1\,000\text{ cm}^3 \) holds \( 1\text{ litre} \) of water, what is the capacity of the basin, to the nearest \( 10^{\text{th}} \) of a litre?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Find the Radius and Halve the Reference Sheet Sphere Formula

First, halve the diameter to find the radius \( r \). Then take half of the sphere volume formula \( V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \) from the Volume and capacity section of the reference sheet:

\[ r = \frac{d}{2} = \frac{50}{2} = 25\text{ cm} \]
\[ V_{\text{hemisphere}} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 \]
Hemisphere (\( \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 \)) Radius (\( r = 25\text{ cm} \)) Removed top half

Step 2: Calculate the Volume of the Basin in \( \text{cm}^3 \)

Substitute \( r = 25\text{ cm} \) into \( V = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 \):

\[ V = \frac{2}{3} \times \pi \times 25^3 \]
\[ V = \frac{2}{3} \times \pi \times 15\,625 = \frac{31\,250\pi}{3} \]
\[ V \approx 32\,724.92\text{ cm}^3 \]

Step 3: Convert to Litres and Round to the Nearest \( 10^{\text{th}} \)

From the reference sheet, \( 1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ mL} \), so \( 1\,000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\,000\text{ mL} = 1\text{ litre} \). Divide the volume in \( \text{cm}^3 \) by \( 1\,000 \):

\[ \text{Capacity} = \frac{32\,724.92\text{ cm}^3}{1\,000} \approx 32.7249\text{ litres} \]
\[ \approx 32.7\text{ litres} \quad (\text{nearest } 10^{\text{th}} \text{ of a litre}) \]

Therefore, Option A is the correct choice.
