Problem

Which of the following could represent the graph of \( y = 4x + 3 \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Compare with Gradient–Intercept Form \( y = mx + c \)

Match \( y = 4x + 3 \) directly with the standard linear equation \( y = mx + c \):

\[ y = \underbrace{4}_{m}x + \underbrace{3}_{c} \]
Gradient: \( m = 4 \) (positive)
\( y \)-intercept: \( c = 3 \) (positive)

Step 2: Interpret What Each Feature Looks Like on a Graph

Positive gradient (\( m = 4 > 0 \)): The line slopes upward as you read from left to right (this eliminates Options C and D).
Positive \( y \)-intercept (\( c = 3 > 0 \)): The line crosses the vertical \( y \)-axis above the origin at \( (0, 3) \) (this eliminates Option A).

Step 3: Match Both Features to the Correct Graph

Option B is the only graph that both rises from left to right and crosses the \( y \)-axis above the origin:

\( y \)-intercept \( (0, 3) > 0 \) Positive gradient (\( m = 4 > 0 \))

Therefore, Option B is the correct choice.
