Problem

Which of the following expressions will \( 4(y - 3) - 5(y + 2) \) correctly simplify to?

\[ 4(y - 3) - 5(y + 2) \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Expand Both Brackets Using the Distributive Law

Multiply every term inside the first bracket by \( 4 \), and every term inside the second bracket by \( -5 \) (making sure the minus sign travels with the \( 5 \)):

Distribute \( +4 \) Distribute \( -5 \) (including minus sign!)
\( 4(y) + 4(-3) + (-5)(y) + (-5)(+2) \)
\[ = 4y - 12 - 5y - 10 \]

Step 2: Group Like Terms Together

Keep each term's sign attached to it, then group the \( y \)-terms together and the constant terms together:

\( 4y \) \( -12 \) \( -5y \) \( -10 \)
Reorder by like terms
\( (4y - 5y) \) \( + \) \( (-12 - 10) \)

Step 3: Combine Like Terms to Simplify

Add the coefficients of \( y \) and combine the two negative constants:

\( 4y - 5y = -y \)   and   \( -12 - 10 = -22 \)
\[ -y - 22 \]

Quick Test (\( y = 0 \)): Original \( = 4(-3) - 5(2) = -12 - 10 = -22 \), matching \( -0 - 22 = -22 \;\checkmark \).

Therefore, Option D is the correct choice.
