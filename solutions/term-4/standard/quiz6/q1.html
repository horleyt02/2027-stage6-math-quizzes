Problem

What is the length of the shortest path from \( F \) to \( B \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Check the Direct "Outer" Routes

Let's intuitively check the most obvious continuous paths along the top and bottom edges of the network.

Top Path: \( F \rightarrow D \rightarrow A \rightarrow B = 13 + 4 + 5 = \mathbf{22} \)
Bottom Path: \( F \rightarrow C \rightarrow B = 6 + 17 = \mathbf{23} \)

Step 2: Check the Middle Routes

Since the direct bottom path has a very heavy edge (17), let's try cutting through the middle to avoid it.

Middle Path: \( F \rightarrow C \rightarrow E \rightarrow B = 6 + 3 + 13 = \mathbf{22} \)

We've found two different routes that equal 22. But can we do better?

Step 3: Finding the Hidden Zig-Zag

In networks, the shortest path sometimes takes a "longer" route in terms of edges, to pick up smaller edge weights. Let's zig-zag through the 3s and 4s.

\[ F \rightarrow C \rightarrow E \rightarrow D \rightarrow A \rightarrow B \] \[ = 6 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 21 \]

21

Therefore, Option B is correct.
