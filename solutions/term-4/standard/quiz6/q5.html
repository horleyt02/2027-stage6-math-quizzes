Problem

Which of the following figures would have the least amount of error if two applications of the trapezoidal rule were used to approximate its shaded area?

Worked Solution

Step 1: The Principle of the Trapezoidal Rule

The trapezoidal rule approximates the area of irregular shapes by constructing trapeziums of equal height.

\[ \text{Approximation} = \text{Area of Trapezoids formed} \]

If the shape does not have straight edges then an error gap is created.

Step 2: Curvature Creates Approximation Errors

When a boundary is curved, replacing it with a straight edge always introduces non-zero error:

  • Concave Up: Straight chords sit above the curve (producing an overestimate).
    Overestimate
  • Concave Down: Straight chords cut below the curve (producing an underestimate).
    Underestimate

Because Figures A, B, and C all possess curved upper boundaries, each will have a definite, non-zero approximation error.

Step 3: Analyze Figure D (Straight Line Segments)

Observe the top boundary of Figure D closely:

Dashed red chords coincide perfectly with the straight edges

Because each strip's upper boundary is already a straight edge, the two trapeziums formed by the trapezoidal rule match the true region identically.

\[ \text{Error} = \text{True Area} - \text{Trapezoidal Area} = \mathbf{0} \]

Figure D (Zero Error)

Therefore, Option D has the least amount of error.
