Problem

The graph of an equation is shown.

Which of the following could be the equation of the line?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Calculate the Gradient (Slope)

The gradient \( m \) of a line passing through two points \( (x_1, y_1) \) and \( (x_2, y_2) \) is calculated using the formula \( m = \frac{\text{Rise}}{\text{Run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \).

\[ m = \frac{4 - (-6)}{5 - (-3)} \]
\[ m = \frac{10}{8} = \mathbf{\frac{5}{4}} \]

This immediately tells us the equation must start with \( y = \frac{5}{4}x \).

Step 2: Calculate the y-intercept

The general equation of a straight line is \( y = mx + c \), where \( c \) is the \(y\)-intercept.

Substitute our gradient \( m = \frac{5}{4} \) and one of our known points (let's use \( (5, 4) \) for easier positive numbers) into the equation to solve for \( c \):

\[ \color{#ef4444}{y} = m\color{#3b82f6}{x} + c \]
\[ \color{#ef4444}{4} = \left(\mathbf{\frac{5}{4}}\right)\color{#3b82f6}{(5)} + c \]
\[ 4 = \frac{25}{4} + c \]

Step 3: Solve for c

Subtract \( \frac{25}{4} \) from both sides. To do this, convert the whole number 4 into a fraction with a denominator of 4:

\[ c = \frac{16}{4} - \frac{25}{4} \]
\[ c = -\frac{9}{4} \]

Step 4: Final Equation

Combine the gradient \( m \) and \(y\)-intercept \( c \) to form the final equation of the line:

\[ y = \frac{5}{4}x - \frac{9}{4} \]

Option B

Therefore, Option B is correct.
