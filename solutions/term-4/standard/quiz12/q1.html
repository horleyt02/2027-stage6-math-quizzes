Problem

This graph shows the relationship between voltage and current in an electrical circuit.

The voltage in the circuit is decreased from 120 volts to 90 volts.

According to the graph, what change in the current occurs as a result?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Read the Graph

First, use the gridlines to find the current at both voltage levels. Each small gridline on the \\(y\\)-axis represents 10V, and each small gridline on the \\(x\\)-axis represents 0.2A.

  • At 120V: The line aligns with 3.2 A (one small line past 3).
  • At 90V: The line aligns with 2.4 A (two small lines past 2).

Step 2: Find the Change

Calculate the absolute change in current, and determine if it is an increase or a decrease.

\( 3.2 \text{ A} - 2.4 \text{ A} = 0.8 \text{ A} \)
Because the value went down, this is a decrease.

Step 3: Calculate Percentage Change

To find the percentage change, divide the difference by the initial starting value, then multiply by 100%.

\[ \text{Percentage Change} = \left( \frac{\text{Change}}{\text{Initial}} \right) \times 100\% \]
\[ = \left( \frac{0.8}{3.2} \right) \times 100\% \]
\[ = 0.25 \times 100\% = 25\% \]

A decrease of 25%

Therefore, Option D is correct.
