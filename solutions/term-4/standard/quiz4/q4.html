Problem

Which of the following graphs represents a dataset in which the mode is larger than both the median and mean?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Identify the Mode

The mode is the most frequent value in a dataset. On a curve or histogram, this always corresponds to the highest peak of the graph.

We are looking for a graph where the highest peak (Mode) is further to the right (larger) than the Mean and Median.

Step 2: Understand the Rule for Skewness

Rule: The Mean is heavily influenced by outliers, so it is always pulled in the direction of the "tail" of the distribution. The Median sits between the Mode and the Mean.

Step 3: Visualizing the Measures

In a negatively skewed distribution, the "tail" extends to the left, containing extreme low values. These low values pull the mean down.

As shown above, the order from smallest to largest along the axis is Mean < Median < Mode. Therefore, the Mode is the largest.

Step 4: Conclusion

We need Mode > Median > Mean (the Mode is the largest/furthest right). This occurs in a negatively skewed distribution (tail on the left, peak on the right).
