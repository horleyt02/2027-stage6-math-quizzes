Problem

Which one of the following graphs could represent the line with equation \( 3x + y = 4 \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Rearrange the Equation

To easily identify the features of the line, rearrange the equation into gradient-intercept form (\( y = mx + c \)).

Subtract \( 3x \) from both sides to isolate \( y \):

\[ y = \mathbf{-3}x \mathbf{+ 4} \]

Step 2: Analyse the y-intercept

In the form \( y = mx + c \), the constant \( c \) is the \( y \)-intercept.

The \( y \)-intercept is \( +4 \).

Because it is positive, the line must cross the vertical \( y \)-axis above the \( x \)-axis.

Step 3: Analyse the Gradient

The coefficient of \( x \), denoted by \( m \), represents the gradient (or slope) of the line.

The gradient is \( -3 \).

Because it is negative, the line must slope downwards from left to right.

Step 4: Match with the graphs

We are looking for a graph that slopes downwards (negative gradient) and crosses the \( y \)-axis above the \( x \)-axis (positive \( y \)-intercept).

  • Graph A has a negative \( y \)-intercept.
  • Graphs B and C slope upwards.
  • Only Graph D matches both features.

Graph D

Therefore, Option D is correct.
