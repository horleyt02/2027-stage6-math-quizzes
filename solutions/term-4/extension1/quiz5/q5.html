Problem

The soccer coach at a school has decided that the intermediate soccer team will consist of eight Year 10 students and six Year 9 students.

After holding tryouts, there are thirteen Year 10 students and eleven Year 9 students who are eligible to join the team.

Which of the following represent the number of ways the team can be selected?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Separate the Selections

Because the team requires specific numbers from specific year groups, we must treat the Year 10 selection and the Year 9 selection as two separate tasks.

Year 10

Choose 8 from 13

\[ {}^{13}C_8 \]

Year 9

Choose 6 from 11

\[ {}^{11}C_6 \]

Step 2: Apply the Fundamental Counting Principle

To form the complete team, the coach must select the Year 10 students AND the Year 9 students.

The 'AND' Rule: When you need one event AND another event to occur together, you multiply their combinations.

Step 3: Combine and Visualise

We multiply the two separate combinations together:

\[ {}^{13}C_8 \times {}^{11}C_6 \]

Option D

Therefore, Option D correctly represents the number of ways.
