Problem

A monic polynomial of degree 4 has one repeated root of multiplicity 2 and is divisible by \( x^2 + 3 \).

Which of the following could be the graph of the polynomial?

Worked Solution

Step 1: End Behavior (Monic Degree 4)

The term 'monic' means the leading coefficient is positive \( (+1) \). Because the degree is 4 (an even number), both ends of the graph must point upwards towards positive infinity as \( x \to \pm\infty \).

This instantly eliminates Graphs A and C, as their ends point downwards.

Step 2: Real vs. Non-Real Roots

The polynomial is divisible by \( x^2 + 3 \). Setting this factor to zero finds its roots:

\[ x^2 + 3 = 0 \]
\[ x^2 = -3 \]

Because you cannot take the square root of a negative number in the real number system, this factor produces no real roots (only non-real roots). Thus, the polynomial cannot have 4 distinct real \( x \)-intercepts.

Step 3: Multiplicity 2 and the Final Graph

The remaining roots come from the "repeated root of multiplicity 2". A multiplicity of 2 means the graph touches the \( x \)-axis at one point and turns around without crossing.

• Graph B crosses the axis twice more, which would mean 4 real roots total. This is impossible due to \( x^2 + 3 \).

• Graph D is the only one that ends upwards, touches once, and has no other \( x \)-intercepts.

Graph D

Therefore, Option D is correct.
