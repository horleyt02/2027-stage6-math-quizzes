Problem

A polynomial \( P(x) \) has degree 4.

When \( P(x) \) is divided by \( D(x) \), the remainder is \( x^2 + 6x + 9 \).

Which of the following is true about the degree of \( D(x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Identify the Degree of the Remainder

Look at the remainder given in the problem:

\[ R(x) = x^2 + 6x + 9 \]

The highest power of \( x \) in this expression is 2, so the degree of the remainder is 2.

Step 2: Apply the Division Theorem

The fundamental rule of polynomial division states that the division process only stops when the degree of the remainder is strictly less than the degree of the divisor:

\[ \deg(R) < \deg(D) \]

Since \( \deg(R) = 2 \), the divisor \( D(x) \) must have a degree of at least 3.

Step 3: Determine Possible Degrees for D(x)

We know \( \deg(D) \ge 3 \). Because the original polynomial \( P(x) \) has degree 4, the divisor can be degree 3 or degree 4 (yielding a quotient of degree 1 or 0 respectively).

Possible Degrees for Divisor D(x)
Because \( \deg(D) \) can be either 3 or 4, it is false to say it must be 3.
However, it could be 3.

Option D

Therefore, Option D is correct.
