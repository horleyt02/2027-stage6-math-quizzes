Problem

The graph of the function \( y = f(x) \) is shown below.

Which of the following graphs most accurately shows the inverse of \( f(x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Map the Intercept

Identify a key point on the original graph \( f(x) \) to see where it moves on the inverse graph.

The graph of \( f(x) \) crosses the \(y\)-axis at a positive value. Let's call this point \( (0, k) \).

Because an inverse function swaps \( x \) and \( y \) coordinates, the new graph must cross the \(x\)-axis at \( (k, 0) \).

Looking at the options, only Graph A has its 'junction' point on the positive \(x\)-axis.

Step 2: Check Direction

Observe the general direction (increasing or decreasing) of the original function.

Notice that the original graph \( f(x) \) is strictly increasing (it always goes up as you move from left to right).

The inverse of a strictly increasing function must also be strictly increasing.

Graph A is the only option that is entirely increasing from left to right.

Step 3: Visual Reflection

Confirm the shape by visualising a reflection across the diagonal line \( y = x \).

  • The steep straight line on the bottom-left becomes a less steep line on the bottom-left.
  • The concave-up curve on the top-right becomes a concave-down curve on the top-right.

Therefore, Option A is correct.
