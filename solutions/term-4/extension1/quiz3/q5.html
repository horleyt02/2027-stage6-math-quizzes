Problem

Which of the following are parametric equations for the following circle?

\[ (x - 7)(x + 1) + (y + 5)(y - 1) = 0 \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Expand and group terms

First, expand the brackets and group the \( x \) and \( y \) terms, moving constants to the right side:

\[ (x^2 - 6x - 7) + (y^2 + 4y - 5) = 0 \] \[ (x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 12 \]

Step 2: Complete the square

Complete the square for both variables. Halve the linear coefficients (\( -6 \) and \( 4 \)), square them, and add them to both sides to keep the equation balanced.

\[ (x^2 - 6x \textcolor{#2563eb}{+ 9}) + (y^2 + 4y \textcolor{#dc2626}{+ 4}) = 12 \textcolor{#2563eb}{+ 9} \textcolor{#dc2626}{+ 4} \] \[ (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25 \]

The centre of our circle is \( (3, -2) \) and the radius squared is \( 25 \).

Step 3: Relate to the Pythagorean Identity

To find the parametric equations, divide both sides by 25 to equal 1:

\[ \frac{(x - 3)^2}{25} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{25} = 1 \]
\[ \left(\frac{x - 3}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{y + 2}{5}\right)^2 = 1 \]

We compare this directly to the fundamental trigonometric identity \( \mathbf{\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1} \).

Step 4: Solve for Parametric forms

By comparing terms, we can set up two separate equations and solve for \( x \) and \( y \):

For \( x \):

\[ \frac{x - 3}{5} = \cos\theta \]

\[ x - 3 = 5\cos\theta \]

\[ x = 3 + 5\cos\theta \]

For \( y \):

\[ \frac{y + 2}{5} = \sin\theta \]

\[ y + 2 = 5\sin\theta \]

\[ y = -2 + 5\sin\theta \]

Option B

Therefore, Option B is correct.
