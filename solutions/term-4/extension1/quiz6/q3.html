Problem

Which of the following is equal to the expression?

\[ \sin x \cos^3 x - \sin^3 x \cos x \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Factor the Expression

Look for the highest common factor (HCF) in both terms of \( \sin x \cos^3 x - \sin^3 x \cos x \).

Both terms share at least one \( \sin x \) and one \( \cos x \). Factoring out \( \sin x \cos x \) gives:

\[ \sin x \cos x (\cos^2 x - \sin^2 x) \]

Step 2: Reference Sheet & Compound Angles

Consult the Compound angles section on your reference sheet to derive the double-angle formulas:

From Reference Sheet:

\[ \sin(A + B) = \sin A \cos B + \cos A \sin B \]
\[ \cos(A + B) = \cos A \cos B - \sin A \sin B \]

Let A = x and B = x:

\( \sin(2x) = 2 \sin x \cos x \implies \sin x \cos x = \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \)
\( \cos(2x) = \cos x \cos x - \sin x \sin x \implies \cos^2 x - \sin^2 x = \cos 2x \)

Substitute these back into our factored expression:

\[ \left( \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \right) ( \cos 2x ) = \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \cos 2x \]

Step 3: Apply the Identity Again

We have \( \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \cos 2x \). Notice that \( \sin 2x \cos 2x \) perfectly matches the structure of the sine double-angle identity again, but with an angle of \( 2x \).

Using \( \sin A \cos A = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2A) \), where \( A = 2x \):

\[ \sin 2x \cos 2x = \frac{1}{2} \sin(4x) \]
Substitute this into our full expression:
\[ \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{2} \sin 4x \right) = \frac{1}{4} \sin 4x \]

\[ \frac{1}{4} \sin 4x \]

Therefore, Option A is correct.
