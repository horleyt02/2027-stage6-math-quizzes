Problem

What is the value of the term independent of \(x\) in the following expansion?

\[ \left(x + \frac{3}{x^2}\right)^6 \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: The Reference Sheet Formula

Look at the binomial expansion formula provided on the reference sheet:

\[ (x + y)^n = ^nC_0 x^n + ^nC_1 x^{n-1}y + ^nC_2 x^{n-2}y^2+ \dots + \dots + ^nC_n y^n \]

From this pattern, we can see that any single term in the expansion takes the form \( ^nC_r (\text{first term})^{n-r} (\text{second term})^r \).

Step 2: Create a General Term Expression

For our specific problem, we identify the components:

  • \( n = 6 \)
  • First term = \( x \)
  • Second term = \( \frac{3}{x^2} \) or \( 3x^{-2} \)

Substitute these into our single term pattern:

\[ ^6C_r (x)^{6-r} (3x^{-2})^r \]

Step 3: Group the Indices

Expand the brackets to separate the constant numbers from the \(x\) variables, then group the \(x\) powers together:

\[ = ^6C_r \times x^{6-r} \times 3^r \times x^{-2r} \]
Multiply the \(x\) terms by adding their powers: \((6-r) + (-2r)\)
\[ = ^6C_r \times 3^r \times x^{6-3r} \]

Step 4: Solve for the Independent Term

A term "independent of \(x\)" means it has no \(x\) variable, which equates to \( x^0 \) (since \( x^0 = 1 \)). Therefore, we set the power of \(x\) to 0 and solve for \(r\):

\[ 6 - 3r = 0 \] \[ 3r = 6 \implies r = 2 \]

Step 5: Calculate the Coefficient

Substitute \( r = 2 \) back into the number part of our general term (\( ^6C_r \times 3^r \)) to find the final value:

\[ ^6C_2 \times 3^2 \]
\[ 15 \times 9 \]
\[ = 135 \]

135

Therefore, Option C is correct.
