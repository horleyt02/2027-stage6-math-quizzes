Problem

A curve is represented by the following parametric equations.

\( x = t - \frac{1}{t} \)
\( y = t^2 + \frac{1}{t^2} \)

Which of the following is the cartesian equation of the curve?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Square the x equation

Looking at the equations, \( y \) contains squared terms (\( t^2 \) and \( \frac{1}{t^2} \)). To relate \( x \) to \( y \), we should square both sides of the \( x \) equation.

\[ x^2 = \left(t - \frac{1}{t}\right)^2 \]

Step 2: Expand carefully

Expand the perfect square using the rule \( (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \). Do not forget the middle term!

\[ x^2 = (t)^2 - 2(t)\left(\frac{1}{t}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^2 \]
Notice how the \( t \) and \( \frac{1}{t} \) in the middle term multiply to give 1:
\[ x^2 = t^2 \textcolor{#ef4444}{- 2} + \frac{1}{t^2} \]

Step 3: Relate back to y

Rearrange the terms slightly to expose the expression for \( y \):

\[ x^2 = \left(t^2 + \frac{1}{t^2}\right) \color{#ef4444}{\mathbf{- 2}} \]
Since we know \( y = t^2 + \frac{1}{t^2} \), we substitute \( y \) in:
\[ x^2 = y - 2 \]

Step 4: Isolate y

To write the final Cartesian equation in standard form, rearrange to make \( y \) the subject. Add 2 to both sides:

\[ y = x^2 + 2 \]

Option C

Therefore, Option C is correct.
