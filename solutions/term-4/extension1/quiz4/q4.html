Problem

Which of the following is equivalent to the following?

\[ \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + x\right) - \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - x\right) \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Use the Reference Sheet

Begin by locating the compound angle formulas on your reference sheet:

\[ \sin(A + B) = \sin A \cos B + \cos A \sin B \]

Step 2: Expand the inner brackets

Apply the formula to expand the inner expressions, knowing that \( \sin(\frac{\pi}{3}) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \) and \( \cos(\frac{\pi}{3}) = \frac{1}{2} \):

\[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + x\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x + \frac{1}{2}\sin x \]
\[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - x\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x - \frac{1}{2}\sin x \]

Step 3: Substitute and Simplify Algebraically

To make the algebra easier, let \( a = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x \) and \( b = \frac{1}{2}\sin x \).

The original expression \( \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + x\right) - \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - x\right) \) becomes:

\[ (a + b)^2 - (a - b)^2 \]
Expand the perfect squares:
\[ (a^2 + 2ab + b^2) - (a^2 - 2ab + b^2) \]
\[ a^2 + 2ab + b^2 - a^2 + 2ab - b^2 \]
\[ = 4ab \]

Step 4: Final Substitution

Substitute your expressions for \( a \) and \( b \) back into \( 4ab \):

\[ 4 \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x\right) \left(\frac{1}{2}\sin x\right) \]
\[ 4 \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\sin x \cos x\right) \]
\[ \sqrt{3} \sin x \cos x \]

Therefore, Option D is correct.

Alternative Method: Difference of Two Squares

You can factorise the entire expression initially using \( A^2 - B^2 = (A-B)(A+B) \):

\[ \left[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + x\right) - \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - x\right) \right] \left[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} + x\right) + \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - x\right) \right] \]

When you expand the inner terms, many cancel out immediately, elegantly leaving \( (2 \times \frac{1}{2}\sin x)(2 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x) \), which multiplies directly to \( \sqrt{3}\sin x\cos x \).
