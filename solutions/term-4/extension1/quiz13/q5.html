Problem

A netball team consisting of \( 10 \) players and \( 3 \) coaches are seated at a circular table for the annual awards night.

In how many ways can the team sit at the table if all the coaches must sit together?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Group the Coaches into One Block

Since all \( 3 \) coaches must sit together, treat them as a single grouped unit (1 block). Together with the \( 10 \) individual players, we have \( 10 + 1 = 11 \) units to seat around the circular table.

10 Players (P1–P10) 3 Coaches (1 Block)

Step 2: Arrange the 11 Units Around the Circular Table

The number of ways to arrange \( n \) distinct items in a circle is \( (n - 1)! \) because rotating the entire table does not create a new seating arrangement.

With \( n = 11 \) units around the circle:
\[ (11 - 1)! = 10! \text{ ways} \]

Step 3: Arrange the Coaches Internally and Multiply

Inside the coaches' block, the \( 3 \) coaches can sit in any order along their \( 3 \) seats. Because their positions relative to the players matter, this is a linear arrangement of \( 3 \) people:

\[ \text{Internal arrangements of coaches} = 3! \]
By the Fundamental Counting Principle, multiply both counts:
\[ \text{Total ways} = 10! \times 3! = 10!\,3! \]

Therefore, Option B is the correct choice.
