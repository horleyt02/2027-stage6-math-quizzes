Problem

Which of the following represents the graph of \( y = x(x - 3)^3(x + 1)^2(4 - x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Determine the Leading Term & End Behaviour

Multiply the highest-power term from each factor to find the leading term of the polynomial:

\[ \text{Leading term} = x \cdot (x)^3 \cdot (x)^2 \cdot (-x) = -x^7 \]
Degree \( 7 \) (odd): The two ends point in opposite directions (eliminating Options A and B).
Negative leading coefficient (\( -1 \)): As \( x \to -\infty, \; y \to +\infty \) (starts high on the left) and as \( x \to +\infty, \; y \to -\infty \) (finishes low on the right).

Step 2: Analyse the Roots (\( x \)-intercepts) & Multiplicities

Set each factor equal to zero and inspect its exponent (multiplicity):

\( x = -1 \) from \( (x + 1)^2 \): Double root (even) — the curve touches and bounces off the \( x \)-axis.
\( x = 0 \) from \( x \): Single root — the curve cuts straight through the origin \( (0, 0) \).
\( x = 3 \) from \( (x - 3)^3 \): Triple root — the curve crosses with a horizontal point of inflection.
\( x = 4 \) from \( (4 - x) \): Single root — the curve cuts straight through \( (4, 0) \).

Step 3: Combine All Features to Identify the Graph

Starting from \( +\infty \) on the left, the graph bounces at \( -1 \), crosses downward through \( 0 \), inflects upward through \( 3 \), and crosses downward through \( 4 \) toward \( -\infty \):

Bounce (\(-1\)) Origin (\(0\)) Inflection (\(3\)) Cross (\(4\))

Therefore, Option C is the correct choice.
