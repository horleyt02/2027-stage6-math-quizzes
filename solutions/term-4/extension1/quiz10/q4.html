Problem

A function \( y = f(x) \) is shown below, with its domain limited to \( x \ge 2 \), so that it will have an inverse.

The line \( y = x \) is also shown.

Which graph shows the inverse function?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Identify key points on f(x)

Look at the solid curve for \( f(x) \). Let's pick some clear coordinate points that it passes through.

The vertex (starting point) is at \( (2, 0) \).

Another clear point on the graph is \( (4, 4) \).

Step 2: Swap the coordinates

To find the points on the inverse function \( f^{-1}(x) \), we swap the \(x\) and \(y\) values of our original points.

\( (2, 0) \) \( (0, 2) \)
\( (4, 4) \) \( (4, 4) \)

This tells us the inverse graph must start at \( (0, 2) \).

Step 3: Visual Reflection

Graphically, an inverse function is a reflection across the line \( y = x \). If you fold the graph along that dashed line, the solid curve should map perfectly onto the inverse curve.

Graph A is the only option that starts at \( (0, 2) \) and mirrors the shape of \( f(x) \) perfectly across the diagonal line.

Therefore, Option A is correct.
