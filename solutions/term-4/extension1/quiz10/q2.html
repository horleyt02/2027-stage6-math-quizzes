Problem

The graphs of \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \) are shown.

Which of the following represents the graph of \( h(x) = \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Check the Origin (x = 0)

First, evaluate both functions at \( x = 0 \) to determine the domain of \( h(x) \).

\( f(0) = 0 \) and \( g(0) = 0 \)
\( h(0) = \frac{0}{0} \) (Undefined)

Because it is an indeterminate form, the graph of \( h(x) \) should have a hole at the origin, which all options correctly display.

Step 2: Analyse the Roots

A fraction \( \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \) can only equal zero if the numerator \( f(x) = 0 \) (and the denominator is not zero).

Looking at the graph of \( f(x) \), its only root is at \( x = 0 \). Therefore, \( h(x) \) cannot have any \(x\)-intercepts. This immediately eliminates Option C, which shows a false root near \( x = -0.8 \).

Step 3: Pointwise Division

To differentiate between the remaining graphs, perform pointwise division at a specific test point, such as \( x = 1.5 \).

  • From the graphs, \( f(1.5) \approx 2.5 \) (or slightly higher).
  • From the graphs, \( g(1.5) \approx 1.2 \).
\[ h(1.5) = \frac{f(1.5)}{g(1.5)} \approx \frac{2.5}{1.2} \approx 2.1 \]

At \( x = 1.5 \), Graph D is too low (barely 1), while Graph B correctly passes through roughly \( y = 2.1 \).

Therefore, Option B is correct.
