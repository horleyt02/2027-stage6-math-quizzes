Problem

The graphs of \( y = f(x) \) and \( y = g(x) \) are shown on the set of axes below.

Which diagram below shows the graph of \( y = f(x) + g(x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Analyse End Behavior

Look at the outer edges of the graphs. \( f(x) \) is a cubic polynomial (starts low on the left, goes high on the right), and \( g(x) \) is a quadratic parabola. Because cubic terms grow much faster than quadratic terms, the cubic function \( f(x) \) will dominate the sum as \( x \) becomes very large.

The combined graph must approach \( +\infty \) on the right side. This eliminates Option A (which goes down) and Option D (which has rational asymptotes).

Step 2: Analyse the X-Intercepts

Notice that \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \) intersect the \(x\)-axis at the exact same two points (the shared roots). At these specific \( x \)-values, both functions equal zero.

If \( f(x_1) = 0 \) and \( g(x_1) = 0 \),
then \( f(x_1) + g(x_1) = 0 + 0 = 0 \)

The new graph must also cross the \(x\)-axis at these exact same two locations. Options B and C both meet this criteria.

Step 3: Analyse the Y-Intercept

To distinguish between the remaining options, evaluate the graphs at \( x = 0 \) (the \(y\)-axis). Observe the original diagram:

  • \( f(0) = 0\).
  • \( g(0) \) is a negative value.
Adding zero to a negative number results in a negative number. Therefore, the \(y\)-intercept of \( y = f(x) + g(x) \) must be negative.

Step 4: Conclusion

Looking at the remaining options at the \(y\)-axis:

  • Option B passes through the origin \( (y=0) \).
  • Option C has a negative \(y\)-intercept.

Option C

Therefore, Option C is the correct graph.
