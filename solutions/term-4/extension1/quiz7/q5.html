Problem

Consider the graph below.

Which parametric equations define the curve shown above?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Identify the Base Shape

Look at the parametric equations provided in the options:

\( x(t) = 2\cos t \)
\( y(t) = 2\sin t \)

Using the identity \( \cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1 \), we can see these equations represent a circle with a radius of \( r = 2 \), centred at the origin.

Step 2: Relate the Parameter to an Angle

In standard trigonometric parametric equations, the parameter \( t \) acts exactly like the angle \( \theta \) on the unit circle, starting from the positive \(x\)-axis and rotating anti-clockwise.

Step 3: Determine the Domain

The graph shows the left semi-circle. This portion of the circle covers Quadrant II and Quadrant III.

  • The top of the circle (positive \(y\)-axis) corresponds to an angle of \( \frac{\pi}{2} \).
  • The curve sweeps through the left side.
  • The bottom of the circle (negative \(y\)-axis) corresponds to an angle of \( \frac{3\pi}{2} \).

Step 4: Select the Correct Option

Therefore, the parameter \( t \) must be restricted to the interval from \( \frac{\pi}{2} \) to \( \frac{3\pi}{2} \).

\[ \frac{\pi}{2} \le t \le \frac{3\pi}{2} \]

Option A

Therefore, Option A is correct.
