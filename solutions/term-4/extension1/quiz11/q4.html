Problem

A curve is defined by a pair of parametric equations given below.

\[ \begin{cases} x = 2\sin t \\ y = \cos t + 2 \end{cases} \text{ for } \frac{\pi}{2} \le t \le \frac{3\pi}{2} \]

Which graph best represents this curve?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Test the Lower Bound

The most reliable way to identify a parametric graph is to test the coordinates at the boundary values of the given domain. The lower bound is \( t = \frac{\pi}{2} \).

Substitute \( t = \frac{\pi}{2} \):
\( x = 2\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}) = 2(1) = 2 \)
\( y = \cos(\frac{\pi}{2}) + 2 = 0 + 2 = 2 \)
The curve must start at the point (2, 2).

Step 2: Test the Upper Bound

Now, test the coordinate at the upper bound of the domain, \( t = \frac{3\pi}{2} \).

Substitute \( t = \frac{3\pi}{2} \):
\( x = 2\sin(\frac{3\pi}{2}) = 2(-1) = -2 \)
\( y = \cos(\frac{3\pi}{2}) + 2 = 0 + 2 = 2 \)
The curve must end at the point (-2, 2).

Step 3: Check the Options

Look for the graph that connects the points \( (2, 2) \) and \( (-2, 2) \).

Option B is the only graph that features both \( (2, 2) \) and \( (-2, 2) \) as its endpoints.

(Note: As a final check, testing the midpoint \( t = \pi \) gives \( x = 0 \) and \( y = 1 \), perfectly confirming the minimum point shown in Graph B).

Therefore, Option B is correct.
