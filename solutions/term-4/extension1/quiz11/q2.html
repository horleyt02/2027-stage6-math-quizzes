Problem

The graph of the function \( f(x) \) is shown.

Which of the following best represents the graph of \( y = |f(x)| \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Define the Transformation

The absolute value function \( |f(x)| \) makes all output values positive.

Geometrically:

  • Any part of the original graph that is above the \(x\)-axis stays exactly where it is.
  • Any part of the original graph that is below the \(x\)-axis is reflected upwards across the \(x\)-axis.

Step 2: Analyse the Original Graph

Identify which parts of the original \( f(x) \) graph are negative.

There are three sections below the x-axis:

  • The far-left tail descending downwards.
  • A small 'dip' (valley) in the middle that crosses the \(y\)-axis.
  • The far-right tail descending downwards.

Step 3: Apply the Reflection

Reflect those three negative sections upwards.

The two large positive 'humps' (peaks) will stay exactly the same. The two downward tails will flip to point upwards. The small negative dip in the middle must reflect upwards to become a new, small positive peak.

Option D is the only graph that correctly shows this result, preserving the roots and the original positive peaks while flipping all negative regions.

Therefore, Option D is correct.
