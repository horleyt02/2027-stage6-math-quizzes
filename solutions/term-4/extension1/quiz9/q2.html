Problem

The diagram below shows the graph of \( y = f(x) \).

Which of the following best represents the graph of \( y^2 = f(|x|) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Analyse \( f(|x|) \)

The absolute value inside the function, \( f(|x|) \), means that negative \( x \)-values produce the same outputs as their positive counterparts.

This reflects the right side of the graph (\( x \ge 0 \)) across the \(y\)-axis, overwriting the original left side. The new function \( f(|x|) \) is positive for \( x \le -2 \) and \( x \ge 2 \), negative in between, and approaches a horizontal asymptote of \( y = 4 \) in both directions.

Step 2: Analyse the \( y^2 \) transformation

The equation \( y^2 = f(|x|) \) can be rewritten as \( y = \pm\sqrt{f(|x|)} \). This has two major effects:

  • Domain restriction: We can only take the square root of non-negative numbers. So, the graph only exists where \( f(|x|) \ge 0 \), which is \( x \le -2 \) and \( x \ge 2 \).
  • Symmetry: The \( \pm \) creates a mirrored reflection across the \(x\)-axis, mapping every positive root to a corresponding negative root.

Step 3: Find the new asymptotes

Evaluate the limits of the new function to find its end behavior.

As \( x \to \pm\infty \), the inner function \( f(|x|) \to 4 \).
Therefore, \( y \to \pm\sqrt{4} = \pm 2 \).

The graph must have horizontal asymptotes at \( y = 2 \) and \( y = -2 \). Option A is the only graph that satisfies the domain, the symmetries, and the new asymptotes.

Option A

Therefore, Option A is correct.
