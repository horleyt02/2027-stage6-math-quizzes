Problem

The graph of the function \( f(x) \) is shown below.

Which of the following best represents the graph of \( (f(x))^2 \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Check the y-intercept

Look at the original graph. The \(y\)-intercept is at \( (0, -2) \).

When we square the function, we square the \(y\)-values. Therefore, the new \(y\)-intercept will be at:

\( (-2)^2 = 4 \)

Only Options A and D have a positive \(y\)-intercept.

Step 2: Check the roots

The original graph has roots (crosses the \(x\)-axis) where \( f(x) = 0 \).

Squaring these values gives \( 0^2 = 0 \). Therefore, the new graph must still touch the \(x\)-axis at these exact same \(x\)-values.

However, since any real number squared is non-negative (\( f(x)^2 \ge 0 \)), the graph cannot go below the \(x\)-axis. It must "bounce" off the axis, turning the roots into local minima.

Step 3: Check the maxima

The original graph has local maxima at \( y = 2 \).

Squaring these gives \( 2^2 = 4 \).

So, the new graph should have peaks reaching up to \( y = 4 \).

Step 4: Check Smoothness

The original function is a smooth wave (like a trigonometric function or polynomial). Squaring a smooth function results in another smooth function.

  • Option A is smooth, reaches 4, and bounces at the roots.
  • Option D has sharp corners (cusps) at the roots, which occurs when taking the absolute value \( |f(x)| \), not squaring.

Option A

Therefore, Option A is correct.
