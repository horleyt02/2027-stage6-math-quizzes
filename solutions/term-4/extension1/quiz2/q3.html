Problem

Which of the following domains would allow \( f(x) = \sqrt{9 - x^2} \) to have an inverse function?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Identify the Graph

The equation \( y = \sqrt{9 - x^2} \) comes from the circle equation \( x^2 + y^2 = 9 \). Because of the positive square root, this represents the top half of a circle with a radius of \( 3 \).

Step 2: The Horizontal Line Test

For a function to have an inverse, it must be one-to-one (it must pass the horizontal line test). If we look at the full natural domain \( -3 \le x \le 3 \), it fails this test because a horizontal line cuts the graph twice.

Step 3: Restrict the Domain

To make the function one-to-one, we must restrict the domain to just one half of the semi-circle (either the left side or the right side). Let's restrict it to the left side.

Step 4: Conclusion

Looking at our options, restricting the domain to the left half means taking all \(x\) values from \(-3\) up to \(0\), inclusive of the endpoints.

\( -3 \le x \le 0 \)

Therefore, Option D is correct.
