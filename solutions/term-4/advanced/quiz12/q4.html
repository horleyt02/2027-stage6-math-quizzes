Problem

Which of the following is an example of a continuous random variable?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Define Continuous vs. Discrete

To identify the correct variable, we need to distinguish between discrete and continuous data.

  • Discrete Variables are usually counted in distinct, whole units (e.g., 1, 2, 3 cars). You cannot have half a car.
  • Continuous Variables are measured and can take on any real value within a range (including fractions or infinite decimals).

Step 2: Analyse the "Counted" Options

Let's look at Options B, C, and D.

  • You count the number of customers (1, 2, 3... customers).
  • You count the number of outcomes (1, 2, 3... outcomes).
  • Frequency is literally a count of how many times something happens.
Because these are all counted in whole numbers, they are all discrete random variables.

Step 3: Analyze the "Measured" Option

Let's look at Option A: Time.

Time is a quantity that we measure, not count.
A bulb could burn out in exactly 10 hours, 10.5 hours, or even 10.5329... hours. Because it can take any infinite number of fractional values along a timeline, it is continuous.

Therefore, Option A is correct.
