Problem

The following function has a vertical and horizontal asymptote.

\[ y = \frac{1}{x + 2} - 4 \]

The graph is reflected in the \( y \)-axis, what are the new asymptotes?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Find the Original Asymptotes

Analyse the original equation \( y = \frac{1}{x + 2} - 4 \) to find its initial boundaries.

Vertical Asymptote (VA): Set the denominator to zero. \( x + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2 \).
Horizontal Asymptote (HA): The vertical shift at the end of the equation. \( y = -4 \).

Step 2: Apply the Reflection Rule

Reflecting a graph in the \(y\)-axis flips it horizontally. Algebraically, this means replacing every \( x \) with \( -x \).

Geometrically, horizontal lines (\(y = c\)) will not change when flipped left-to-right.
Vertical lines (\(x = c\)) will flip to the opposite side of the y-axis (\(x = -c\)).

Step 3: Determine New Asymptotes

Apply the rule from Step 2 to our original asymptotes.

New VA: \( x = -2 \) flips to become \( x = 2 \).
New HA: \( y = -4 \) is a horizontal line, so it stays \( y = -4 \).

Therefore, Option D is correct.
