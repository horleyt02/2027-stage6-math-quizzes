Problem

The equation \( x^2 + y^2 + 8x - 6y + 10 = 0 \) represents a circle.

What are the coordinates of the centre of the circle?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Group x and y terms

Rearrange the given equation to group the \(x\) terms together and the \(y\) terms together, moving the constant to the right side:

\[ (x^2 + 8x) + (y^2 - 6y) = -10 \]

Step 2: Complete the Square

Halve the coefficients of \(x\) and \(y\), square them, and add them to both sides of the equation to maintain balance.

  • For \(x\): \( \left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2 = 4^2 = 16 \)
  • For \(y\): \( \left(\frac{-6}{2}\right)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9 \)
\[ (x^2 + 8x + 16) + (y^2 - 6y + 9) = -10 + 16 + 9 \]

Step 3: Factor into Perfect Squares

Rewrite the grouped quadratic expressions as perfect squares and simplify the right side:

\[ (x + 4)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 15 \]

Step 4: Use the Reference Sheet Formula

The standard formula for a circle from the reference sheet is:

\[ (x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2 \]

The centre of the circle is at \( (a, b) \). Rewrite our equation to match this format explicitly using subtraction:

\[ (x - (-4))^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 15 \]

From this, we can see \( a = -4 \) and \( b = 3 \).

Centre: \( (-4, 3) \)

Therefore, Option A is correct.
