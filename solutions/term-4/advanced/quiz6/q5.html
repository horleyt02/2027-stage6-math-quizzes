Problem

The displacement of a particle, in centimetres, is given by

\[ s = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 \]

where \( t \) is in seconds.

Which of the following statements is true?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Differentiate to Find Velocity and Acceleration

Differentiating displacement \( s(t) \) with respect to time gives velocity, and differentiating again gives acceleration:

Displacement: \[ s = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 \]
Velocity: \[ v = \frac{ds}{dt} = 6t - 12 = 6(t - 2) \]
Acceleration: \[ a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \mathbf{6\text{ cm/s}^2} \]

Step 2: Evaluate Acceleration (Statement D)

Notice the expression for acceleration:

\[ a(t) = 6\text{ cm/s}^2 \quad (\text{independent of } t) \]

Because \( a = 6 \) contains no variable \( t \), the particle experiences a uniform, unchanging acceleration of \( 6\text{ cm/s}^2 \) for all times.

Statement D is unequivocally TRUE.

Step 3: 1D Physical Motion Path (Disproving A, B, and C)

To understand why statements A, B, and C are false, trace the particle's physical path along the 1D displacement line:

1D Horizontal Motion Timeline
• Why A is False: For \( t > 2 \), velocity is positive (\( v > 0 \)), so the particle moves to the right; its displacement is increasing.
• Why B is False: At \( t = 2 \), \( v = 0 \) and velocity changes from negative to positive, so the particle changes direction.
• Why C is False: At \( t = 2 \), \( s = -3\text{ cm} < 0 \), so displacement is not always positive.

Option D

Therefore, Option D is the only true statement.
