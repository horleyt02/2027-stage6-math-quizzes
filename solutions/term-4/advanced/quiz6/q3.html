Problem

What is the domain of the following?

\[ y = \sqrt{4x - 9} \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: The Non-Negative Radicand Condition

The domain of a function is the set of all allowable real input values (\( x \)). For real values of \( y \), the quantity under a square root (the radicand) cannot be negative:

\[ \text{Radicand} \ge 0 \implies 4x - 9 \ge 0 \]

Notice the inequality is greater than or equal to zero (\( \ge \)) because \( \sqrt{0} = 0 \), which is a completely valid real number.

Step 2: Solve the Linear Inequality

Isolate \( x \) using standard inverse operations:

  • Add \( 9 \) to both sides: \( 4x \ge 9 \)
  • Divide both sides by \( 4 \): \( x \ge \frac{9}{4} \)
\[ x \ge \frac{9}{4} = 2.25 = \mathbf{2\frac{1}{4}} \]

Step 3: Convert to Interval Notation & Visualise

Because the inequality includes the endpoint (\( \ge \)), it must be represented with a solid closed circle on a number line and a square bracket in interval notation:

Correct Number Line Representation
\[ \text{Domain: } \mathbf{\left[2\frac{1}{4}, \infty\right)} \]

\(\left[2\frac{1}{4}, \infty\right)\)
