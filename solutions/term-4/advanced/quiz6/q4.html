Problem

To obtain the graph of \( y = -3(x + 1)^2 + 5 \) from \( y = x^2 \), which sequence of transformations can be applied?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Horizontal Translation (Inside the Bracket)

We start with the base parabola \( y = x^2 \). To affect horizontal movement, we replace \( x \):

Replace \( x \) with \( (x + 1) \):
\[ y = (x + 1)^2 \]

Replacing \( x \) with \( x + c \) where \( c > 0 \) shifts the graph left by 1 unit (the new vertex shifts from \( (0,0) \) to \( (-1, 0) \)).

Step 2: Vertical Dilation & Reflection

Next, consider the coefficient \( -3 \) applied to the squared term:

• Dilation: Replace \( y \) with \( \frac{y}{3} \):
\[ \frac{y}{3} = (x + 1)^2 \implies y = 3(x + 1)^2 \]
This represents a vertical dilation by a factor of 3 (all \( y \)-values are stretched by 3 away from the \( x \)-axis).
• Reflection: Replace \( y \) with \( -y \):
\[ -y = 3(x + 1)^2 \implies y = -3(x + 1)^2 \]
Negating the \( y \)-values inverts the curve across the horizontal axis: reflection in the \( x \)-axis.

Step 3: Vertical Translation & Full Transformation Visual

To shift the graph vertically upwards by 5 units, we replace \( y \) with \( (y - 5) \):

Replace \( y \) with \( (y - 5) \):
\[ (y - 5) = -3(x + 1)^2 \]
Transposing \( -5 \) to the other side:
\[ y = -3(x + 1)^2 + 5 \]

Option A

Therefore, Option A accurately lists all four transformations in proper order.
