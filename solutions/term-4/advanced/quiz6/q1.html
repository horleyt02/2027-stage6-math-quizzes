Problem

Which of the following is equal to

\[ 1 + \cot^2(3x) \text{ ?} \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Check the Reference Sheet Identities

From the Trigonometric identities section on your reference sheet:

From the Reference Sheet:
\[ \operatorname{cosec} A = \frac{1}{\sin A}, \quad \cot A = \frac{\cos A}{\sin A}, \quad \cos^2 x + \sin^2 x = 1 \]

Divide the fundamental identity \( \sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1 \) through by \( \sin^2 A \):

\[ \frac{\sin^2 A}{\sin^2 A} + \frac{\cos^2 A}{\sin^2 A} = \frac{1}{\sin^2 A} \]
\[ \mathbf{1 + \cot^2 A = \operatorname{cosec}^2 A} \]

Step 2: Substitute the Argument \( A = 3x \)

In this problem, the angle argument is \( A = 3x \). We substitute \( 3x \) directly into the identity:

\[ 1 + \cot^2(3x) = \mathbf{\operatorname{cosec}^2(3x)} \]

Step 3: Why the 3 Cannot be Factored Out as 9

The coefficient \( 3 \) is an internal property of the angle argument \( (3x) \), not an external multiplier:

\[ \cot^2(3x) \ne 3^2 \cot^2(x) \]
\[ \cot^2(3x) \ne 9 \cot^2(x) \]

Trigonometric functions are non-linear; coefficients inside cannot be factored out or squared outside the function. The argument remains \( 3x \).

\( \operatorname{cosec}^2 3x \)

Therefore, Option A is correct.
