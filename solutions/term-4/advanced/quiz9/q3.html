Problem

Which graph shows the curve \( y = \log_3 (x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Domain & Asymptote

The argument of a logarithmic function must always be strictly positive.

For \( y = \log_3(x) \), we require \( x > 0 \).

This means the entire graph must exist strictly on the right side of the \(y\)-axis, creating a vertical asymptote at \( x = 0 \).

Step 2: Find the Intercept

To find where the graph crosses the \(x\)-axis, set \( y = 0 \):

\[ \log_3(x) = 0 \]
Rewrite in exponential form:
\[ x = 3^0 = 1 \]

The graph must pass exactly through the point \( (1, 0) \).

Step 3: Match the Graph

Evaluate the four graphical options based on our findings:

  • Graph A: On the left side (\( x < 0 \)). Incorrect.
  • Graph B: Has a \(y\)-intercept at 1. Incorrect.
  • Graph C: On the right side, vertical asymptote at \( x=0 \), crosses \(x\)-axis at \( 1 \). Correct!
  • Graph D: Has a \(y\)-intercept at 1. Incorrect.

Option C

Therefore, Option C is correct.
