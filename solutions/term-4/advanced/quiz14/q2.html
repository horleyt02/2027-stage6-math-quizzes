Problem

A semicircle is shown below:

What is the equation of the semicircle?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Write the Equation of the Full Circle

From the graph, the semicircle is centred at the origin \( (0, 0) \) and passes through \( (-3, 0) \), \( (3, 0) \), and \( (0, -3) \), so its radius is \( r = 3 \). Substitute \( r = 3 \) into the standard circle equation \( x^2 + y^2 = r^2 \):

\[ x^2 + y^2 = 3^2 \]
\[ x^2 + y^2 = 9 \]

Step 2: Make \( y \) the Subject to Split into Two Semicircles

Solve \( x^2 + y^2 = 9 \) for \( y \):

\[ y^2 = 9 - x^2 \implies y = \pm\sqrt{9 - x^2} \]

The \( + \) root gives the upper semicircle (\( y \ge 0 \)), while the \( - \) root gives the lower semicircle (\( y \le 0 \)):

Upper: \( y = +\sqrt{9 - x^2} \) Lower: \( y = -\sqrt{9 - x^2} \)

Step 3: Choose the Sign Matching the Given Graph

Because the given semicircle lies entirely on or below the \( x \)-axis (\( y \le 0 \)), we select the negative square root. We can verify with the \( y \)-intercept \( (0, -3) \):

At \( x = 0 \):   \( y = -\sqrt{9 - 0^2} = -\sqrt{9} = -3 \;\checkmark \)
\[ y = -\sqrt{9 - x^2} \]

Therefore, Option D is the correct choice.
