Problem

Consider the following equation for \( -180^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ \):

\[ 2\cos x + 1 = 0 \]

What is the solution of the equation?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Isolate \( \cos x \) and Find the Reference Angle

Rearrange \( 2\cos x + 1 = 0 \) to make \( \cos x \) the subject:

\[ 2\cos x = -1 \implies \cos x = -\frac{1}{2} \]
Ignore the negative sign first to find the acute related angle:
\[ \cos\alpha = \frac{1}{2} \implies \alpha = 60^\circ \]

Step 2: Locate the Quadrants on the ASTC Unit Circle

Since \( \cos x = -\frac{1}{2} < 0 \), the solutions lie in Quadrant 2 and Quadrant 3. Within the required domain \( -180^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ \):

Quadrant 2 (anticlockwise): \( x = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = +120^\circ \)
Quadrant 3 (clockwise): \( x = -(180^\circ - 60^\circ) = -120^\circ \) (or \( 240^\circ - 360^\circ \))
\( +120^\circ \) (Q2) \( -120^\circ \) (Q3) \( \cos x = -\frac{1}{2} \)

Step 3: Verify Graphically on \( -180^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ \)

Plotting \( y = \cos x \) and the horizontal line \( y = -\frac{1}{2} \) across \( [-180^\circ, 180^\circ] \) confirms both symmetric intersections:

\[ x = -120^\circ, \; 120^\circ \]

Therefore, Option A is the correct choice.
