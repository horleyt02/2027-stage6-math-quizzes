Problem

A particle is moving in a straight line.

At time \( t \) seconds it has displacement \( x \) metres from a fixed point \( O \) on the line and velocity \( v\text{ ms}^{-1} \). The displacement is given by:

\[ x = \frac{16t}{2t + 1} \]

What is the time (in seconds) taken for the speed of the particle to equal \( 1\text{ ms}^{-1} \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Find the Velocity \( v = \dfrac{dx}{dt} \) Using the Quotient Rule

Velocity is the derivative of displacement with respect to time. Apply the Quotient Rule \( \dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{u}{w}\right) = \dfrac{wu' - uw'}{w^2} \) with \( u = 16t \) and \( w = 2t + 1 \):

\( u' = 16 \)   and   \( w' = 2 \)
\[ v = \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{(2t + 1)(16) - (16t)(2)}{(2t + 1)^2} \]
\[ v = \frac{32t + 16 - 32t}{(2t + 1)^2} \]
\[ v = \frac{16}{(2t + 1)^2} \]

Step 2: Set Speed Equal to \( 1\text{ ms}^{-1} \) and Solve for \( t \)

Since \( v = \dfrac{16}{(2t + 1)^2} > 0 \) for all \( t \ge 0 \), the speed \( |v| \) is simply equal to \( v \). Set \( v = 1 \):

\[ \frac{16}{(2t + 1)^2} = 1 \implies (2t + 1)^2 = 16 \]
\[ 2t + 1 = \pm 4 \]
\( 2t + 1 = 4 \implies 2t = 3 \implies t = \dfrac{3}{2} \)  (valid, \( t \ge 0 \))
\( 2t + 1 = -4 \implies 2t = -5 \implies t = -\dfrac{5}{2} \)
(rejected since time \( t \ge 0 \))

Step 3: Visualise the Velocity-Time Graph

Starting from an initial velocity of \( v(0) = 16\text{ ms}^{-1} \), the particle decelerates smoothly along \( v = \frac{16}{(2t + 1)^2} \), crossing the horizontal line \( v = 1\text{ ms}^{-1} \) at exactly \( t = \frac{3}{2}\text{ s} \):

\( v = \frac{16}{(2t + 1)^2} \) \( v = 1\text{ ms}^{-1} \) \( \left(\frac{3}{2}, 1\right) \)
\[ t = \frac{3}{2}\text{ seconds} \]

Therefore, Option B is the correct choice.
