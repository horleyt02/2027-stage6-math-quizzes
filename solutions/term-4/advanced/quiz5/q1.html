Problem

What is the \( x \) coordinate of the point/s of intersection of \( y = x^2 + x - 3 \) and \( y = 2x + 3 \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Equate Both Expressions for \( y \)

At points of intersection, both functions share identical \( y \)-values for the same \( x \)-value. Set the two equations equal to each other:

\[ x^2 + x - 3 = 2x + 3 \]

Step 2: Rearrange into Standard Quadratic Form

To solve any quadratic equation, gather all terms onto one side so the equation equals zero (\( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \)):

  • Subtract \( 2x \) from both sides: \( x - 2x = -x \)
  • Subtract \( 3 \) from both sides: \( -3 - 3 = -6 \)
\[ x^2 + x - 2x - 3 - 3 = 0 \]
\[ x^2 - x - 6 = 0 \]

Step 3: Factorise and Apply the Null Factor Law

Find two numbers that multiply to \( -6 \) and add up to \( -1 \). These numbers are \( -3 \) and \( +2 \):

\[ (x - 3)(x + 2) = 0 \]
\[ x = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -2 \]

\( x = -2 \) and \( x = 3 \)

Therefore, Option C is correct.
