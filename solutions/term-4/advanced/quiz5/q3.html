Problem

Which of the following is equal to

\[ \frac{\ln 9}{\ln 5} \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Check the Reference Sheet Formula

On your formula sheet, the Change of Base Rule is given directly as:

From the Reference Sheet:
\[ \log_a x = \frac{\log_b x}{\log_b a} \]

Recall that the natural logarithm \( \ln \) is simply a logarithm with base \( e \) (i.e., \( \ln y = \log_e y \)). Here, our common base is \( b = e \).

Step 2: Map the Numbers to the Formula

Matching the given expression \( \frac{\ln 9}{\ln 5} \) to the right side of the formula \( \frac{\log_b x}{\log_b a} \):

• Top argument: \( x = 9 \) stays the main argument.
• Bottom argument: \( a = 5 \) becomes the new base.

Step 3: Conclude & Contrast with the Quotient Rule

Putting it all together:

\[ \frac{\ln 9}{\ln 5} = \frac{\log_e 9}{\log_e 5} = \mathbf{\log_5 9} \]

Don't confuse with the Quotient Law:

• Quotient Law: \( \ln\left(\frac{9}{5}\right) = \ln 9 - \ln 5 \) (a single log of a fraction).

• Change of Base: \( \frac{\ln 9}{\ln 5} \) is a fraction of two separate logs, which collapses into \( \log_5 9 \).

\( \log_5 9 \)

Therefore, Option D is correct.
