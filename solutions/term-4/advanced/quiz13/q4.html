Problem

Given \( P(A) = 0.3 \), \( P(B) = 0.6 \), and \( P(A \cap B) = 0.2 \), which of the following correctly describes the events \( A \) and \( B \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Test for Mutual Exclusivity

Two events \( A \) and \( B \) are mutually exclusive if they cannot occur at the same time — meaning their intersection must be zero: \( P(A \cap B) = 0 \).

\[ P(A \cap B) = 0.2 \neq 0 \]
Since the overlap is non-zero, \( A \) and \( B \) are non-mutually exclusive.
\( P(A) = 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.3 \) \( P(B) = 0.2 + 0.4 = 0.6 \)

Step 2: Test for Independence

Two events \( A \) and \( B \) are independent if and only if \( P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B) \). Compare the product with the given intersection:

\[ P(A) \times P(B) = 0.3 \times 0.6 = 0.18 \]
\[ P(A \cap B) = 0.2 \]
\[ 0.18 \neq 0.2 \implies P(A \cap B) \neq P(A) \times P(B) \]

Because the two values are not equal, \( A \) and \( B \) are non-independent (dependent).

Step 3: Combine Both Results

Mutually Exclusive? No — \( P(A \cap B) = 0.2 \neq 0 \) (non-mutually exclusive).
Independent? No — \( 0.3 \times 0.6 = 0.18 \neq 0.2 \) (non-independent).

Therefore, Option D is the correct choice.
