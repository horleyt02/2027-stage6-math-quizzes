Problem

The graph shows \( y = ka^x \).

What are the values of \( a \) and \( k \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Substitute Both Points into \( y = ka^x \)

Substitute the two given coordinates \( (1, 14) \) and \( (2, 56) \) into \( y = ka^x \) to form a pair of simultaneous equations:

\[ \text{At } (1, 14): \quad 14 = ka^1 \implies ka = 14 \quad \text{--- (1)} \]
\[ \text{At } (2, 56): \quad 56 = ka^2 \implies ka^2 = 56 \quad \text{--- (2)} \]

Step 2: Divide Equation (2) by Equation (1) to Find \( a \) and \( k \)

Dividing the two equations eliminates \( k \) immediately:

\[ \frac{ka^2}{ka} = \frac{56}{14} \]
\[ a = 4 \]
Substitute \( a = 4 \) back into Equation (1):
\[ 4k = 14 \implies k = \frac{14}{4} \]
\[ k = 3.5 \]

Step 3: Visualise the Growth Factor and \( y \)-Intercept

In \( y = ka^x \), \( k \) is the \( y \)-intercept (when \( x = 0 \)) and \( a \) is the multiplicative growth factor for every \( +1 \) step in \( x \):

\( y \)-intercept \( k = 3.5 \) Growth jumps (\( \times 4 \))

Therefore, \( a = 4 \) and \( k = 3.5 \) (Option D).
