Problem

Consider the following functions which are continuous on the domain \( (-\infty, \infty) \):

\( f(x) \) is an odd function
\( g(x) \) is an even function
\( h(x) \) is neither even nor odd, but is one-to-one

Which of the functions MUST have at least two different tangent lines that are parallel?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Analyse the Odd Function \( f(x) \)

Two tangent lines are parallel when they have the exact same gradient. Since \( f(x) \) is odd, \( f(-x) = -f(x) \). Differentiate both sides using the Chain Rule:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\big[f(-x)\big] = \frac{d}{dx}\big[-f(x)\big] \]
\[ -f'(-x) = -f'(x) \]
\[ f'(-x) = f'(x) \quad \text{(an even derivative!)} \]

Geometric Symmetry: An odd function has \( 180^\circ \) rotational symmetry about the origin. Rotating a tangent line by \( 180^\circ \) preserves its slope, so the tangents at \( x = -a \) and \( x = a \) are always parallel (as seen in \( f(x) = x^3 \)):

Equal gradients at \( x = -a \) and \( x = a \): Parallel tangents!

Step 2: Test the Even Function \( g(x) \)

For an even function, \( g(-x) = g(x) \). Differentiating both sides with the Chain Rule gives:

\[ -g'(-x) = g'(x) \implies g'(-x) = -g'(x) \]

Geometric Symmetry: Reflecting a line across the \( y \)-axis negates its gradient (\( m \to -m \)). In the counterexample \( g(x) = x^2 \), \( g'(x) = 2x \) takes every gradient value only once — no two tangent lines are parallel!

Opposite gradients (\( -m \) vs. \( +m \)): Not parallel!

Step 3: Test the One-to-One Function \( h(x) \) and Conclude

Consider the counterexample \( h(x) = e^x \). It is continuous on \( (-\infty, \infty) \), neither even nor odd, and strictly increasing (one-to-one). Its derivative \( h'(x) = e^x \) is also strictly increasing, meaning every single point has a different gradient:

Strictly increasing gradients (\( m_1 \neq m_2 \)): No parallel tangents!

Only \( f(x) \) is guaranteed to have parallel tangents. Therefore, Option A is the correct choice.
