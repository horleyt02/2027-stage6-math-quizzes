Problem

For functions \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \) it is known that \( f(2) = -1 \), \( f'(2) = 4 \), \( f'(5) = 6 \), \( g(2) = 5 \) and \( g'(2) = 3 \).

When \( x = 2 \), what is the value of the derivative of \( f(g(x)) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Recall the Chain Rule from the Reference Sheet

From the reference sheet, the Chain Rule for a composite function is:

\( y = g(u) \) where \( u = f(x) \implies \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{du} \times \dfrac{du}{dx} \)
Applying this to \( y = f(g(x)) \) by letting \( u = g(x) \) and \( y = f(u) \):
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\big[f(g(x))\big] = \frac{dy}{du} \times \frac{du}{dx} = f'(g(x)) \times g'(x) \]

Step 2: Identify the Needed Values at \( x = 2 \)

Substitute \( x = 2 \) into \( f'(g(x)) \times g'(x) \) to get \( f'(g(2)) \times g'(2) \). Notice that two of the given values are extra distractors:

Needed
\( g(2) = 5 \)
Inner value \( u \)
Needed
\( f'(5) = 6 \)
Outer deriv. \( \frac{dy}{du} \)
Needed
\( g'(2) = 3 \)
Inner deriv. \( \frac{du}{dx} \)
Distractor
\( f(2) = -1 \)
Not needed
Distractor
\( f'(2) = 4 \)
Wrong input (\( 2 \neq 5 \))

Step 3: Substitute and Evaluate

Evaluate the inner function first (\( g(2) = 5 \)), then multiply \( f'(5) \) by \( g'(2) \):

\[ \left.\frac{d}{dx}\big[f(g(x))\big]\right|_{x=2} = f'(g(2)) \times g'(2) \]
\[ = f'(5) \times g'(2) \]
\[ = 6 \times 3 \]
\[ = 18 \]

Therefore, Option D is the correct choice.
