Problem

The graph of a function \( y = f(x) \) is shown below.

It has an asymptote at \( y = -2.5 \).

Which statement correctly describes its domain and range?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Finding the Domain (Left-to-Right)

The domain refers to all possible \( x \)-values for the function. We look at the graph horizontally from left to right.

  • The graph starts at \( x = 2 \). Because there is an open circle at this point, the value 2 is excluded. We use a round parenthesis: \( (2 \).
  • The graph continues infinitely to the right, indicated by the arrow. This goes to \( \infty \).
Domain: \( (2, \infty) \)

Step 2: Finding the Range (Bottom-to-Top)

The range refers to all possible \( y \)-values. We look at the graph vertically from bottom to top to find the absolute minimum and absolute maximum boundaries.

  • The lowest point the graph reaches is at \( y = -3 \). This is an open circle, so -3 is excluded: \( (-3 \).
  • The highest point the graph reaches is the peak at \( y = -1 \). This is a solid dot, meaning -1 is included in the range. We use a square bracket: \( -1] \).
  • Note: The arrow on the right approaches the asymptote \( y = -2.5 \), but since this value falls comfortably inside the interval between -3 and -1, it doesn't change our absolute boundaries.
Range: \( (-3, -1] \)

Step 3: Conclusion

Combining our findings, we have a domain of \( (2, \infty) \) and a range of \( (-3, -1] \).

Option D

Therefore, Option D is correct.
