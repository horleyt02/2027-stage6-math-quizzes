Problem

The Venn diagram below shows the number of members of two groups, the Chess Club and the Football Team, in a class.

If a person is chosen at random from those who are not in the Football Team, what is the probability that they are also not in the Chess Club?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Formal Notation

The phrase "chosen at random from those who are..." tells us this is a conditional probability problem. We are restricting our sample space.

Looking at the reference sheet formula:

\[ P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \]

In practice, when counting from a Venn diagram, it is much easier to just use the raw numbers (counts) in the restricted regions instead of full probabilities: \( \frac{n(A \cap B)}{n(B)} \).

Step 2: Find the Denominator (The Condition)

We are given the condition that the person is not in the Football Team. Let's find our new restricted total by highlighting everyone outside the Football circle.

Total Not Football = \( 9 + 8 = \mathbf{17} \)

Step 3: Find the Numerator (The Target)

Out of these 17 people in our blue shaded area, we want to find those who are also not in the Chess Club.

Since the 9 are in the Chess Club, only the 8 outside both circles satisfy this requirement.

Not Chess \(\cap\) Not Football = \( \mathbf{8} \)

Step 4: Final Calculation

Construct the final probability fraction: \( \frac{\text{Target}}{\text{Condition}} \)

\[ \frac{8}{17} \]

Option C

Therefore, Option C is correct.
