Problem

What are the coordinates of the vertex of the following parabola?

\[ y = (x + 3)^2 + 7 \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: The Vertex Form Formula

The standard "vertex form" of a parabola's equation is given by:

\[ y = a(x - h)^2 + k \]

In this formula, the vertex (the lowest or highest point of the parabola) is located exactly at the coordinates \( (h, k) \).

Step 2: Match the Equations

Let's line up our given equation directly underneath the standard formula to compare the parts. We need to be careful with the signs, specifically rewriting \( (x + 3) \) to match the \( (x - h) \) format.

\[ y = a(x - \mathbf{h})^2 + \mathbf{k} \]
\[ y = 1(x - (\mathbf{-3}))^2 + \mathbf{7} \]

Step 3: Extract the Coordinates

By comparing the two equations, we can extract the exact values for \( h \) (the x-coordinate) and \( k \) (the y-coordinate):

  • Looking at the inside of the parentheses, \( h \) is -3.
  • Looking at the number added outside, \( k \) is 7.
Vertex \( (h, k) \) = \( (-3, 7) \)

Option B

Therefore, Option B is correct.
