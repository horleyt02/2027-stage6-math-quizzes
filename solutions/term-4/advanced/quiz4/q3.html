Problem

If \( y = \sqrt{9 - x^2} \) what is an expression for \( \frac{dy}{dx} \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Rewrite and Reference Formula

First, rewrite the square root as a fractional exponent so we can apply the power rule:

\[ y = (9 - x^2)^{\frac{1}{2}} \]

Now, refer to the formula on your reference sheet for differentiating a function to a power (the Chain Rule):

\[ y = f(x)^n \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = n f'(x) [f(x)]^{n-1} \]

Step 2: Apply the Chain Rule

Let's map our expression to the formula:

  • \( n = \frac{1}{2} \)
  • \( f(x) = 9 - x^2 \)
  • \( f'(x) = -2x \)

Substitute these into the derivative formula:

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \cdot (-2x) \cdot (9 - x^2)^{\frac{1}{2} - 1} \]
\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)(-2x)(9 - x^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \]

Step 3: Simplify

Multiply the coefficients \( \left(\frac{1}{2} \times -2 = -1\right) \) and rewrite the negative fractional exponent as a square root in the denominator:

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = -x(9 - x^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \]
\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-x}{(9 - x^2)^{\frac{1}{2}}} \]
\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{9 - x^2}} \]

Step 4: Substitute y back into the expression

Notice that the denominator of our derivative is exactly the same as our original function: \( y = \sqrt{9 - x^2} \).

We can replace the denominator with \( y \) to match the options:

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y} \]

Option A

Therefore, Option A is correct.
