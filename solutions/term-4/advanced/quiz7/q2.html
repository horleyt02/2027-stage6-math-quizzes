Problem

Which of the following is the solution to the following equation?

\[ 3^{5x+2} = 9^{3x-1} \]

Worked Solution

Step 1: Find a Common Base

To solve equations with variables in the exponent, we need the base numbers on both sides to match. Notice that \( 9 \) is a power of \( 3 \).

We can rewrite \( 9 \) as \( 3^2 \).

Step 2: Apply Exponent Rules

Substitute \( 3^2 \) for \( 9 \) in the equation. Then, use the power of a power rule: \( (x^a)^b = x^{ab} \).

\[ 3^{5x+2} = (\mathbf{3^2})^{3x-1} \]
Distribute the 2 to both terms in the exponent:
\[ 3^{5x+2} = 3^{\mathbf{2}(3x-1)} \]
\[ 3^{5x+2} = 3^{6x-2} \]

Step 3: Equate Exponents and Solve

Since the bases are now exactly the same (\( 3 \)), their exponents must be equal for the equation to be true. Set the exponents equal to each other.

\[ 5x + 2 = 6x - 2 \]
Subtract 5x from both sides, and add 2 to both sides:
\[ 2 + 2 = 6x - 5x \]

\[ x = 4 \]

Therefore, Option B is correct.
