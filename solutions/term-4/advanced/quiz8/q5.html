Problem

The function \( f(x) = x^2 - 1 \) has domain \( (-\infty, \infty) \) and range \( [-1, \infty) \).

The function \( g(x) \) is a transformation of \( f(x) \), such that:

\[ g(x) = -f(x - 4) + 2 \]

What is the range of \( g(x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Separate Domain vs. Range Transformations

Transformations inside the function bracket (like \( x-4 \)) are horizontal and only affect the domain.

To find the new range, we only look at the vertical transformations outside the function:
  • The negative sign: multiply by \( -1 \) (vertical reflection)
  • The \( +2 \): add \( 2 \) (vertical shift)

*Note: Just like normal algebra, we must apply multiplication (the reflection) before addition (the shift).

Step 2: Apply the Vertical Reflection

The original range is \( [-1, \infty) \), which means \( y \ge -1 \).

Multiplying by a negative flips the graph upside down. This changes the minimum value into a maximum, and reverses the inequality.

\( y \ge -1 \)
Multiply by -1 and flip the sign:
\( y \le 1 \)   or   \( (-\infty, 1] \)

Step 3: Apply the Vertical Shift

Finally, take the reflected range and apply the vertical shift by adding 2 to the y-values.

\( y \le 1 \mathbf{+ 2} \)
\( y \le 3 \)

\[ (-\infty, 3] \]

Therefore, Option B is correct.
