Problem

A baker collects data from batches of sourdough bread made in a new oven.

Which of the following is an example of a discrete random variable?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Define Discrete vs. Continuous

To classify the variables, we must understand the difference between the two main types of numerical data.

Continuous Variables are measured. They can take on any value within an interval, meaning there are infinitely many decimal possibilities (e.g., 2.5, 2.51, 2.518...).

Discrete Variables have distinct, separate values. While this usually means they are found by counting whole objects (like 1, 2, 3), it also includes variables that come in specific "steps" (like shoe sizes 7, 7.5, 8 where in-between values like 7.3 don't exist).

Step 2: Analyse the "Measuring" Options

Let's look at Options A, B, and D together.

  • Temperature (A): Measured. It could be 220°C, 220.5°C, 220.53°C, depending on the thermometer's precision.
  • Time (B): Measured. Dough might rise in exactly 2 hours, or 2.15 hours, or 2.1587 hours.
  • Mass (D): Measured. A loaf might weigh 850g, 850.2g, or 850.24g on a sensitive scale.

Because these are all continuous, Options A, B, and D are incorrect.

Step 3: Identify the "Counting" Option

Now let's examine Option C.

The 'number of loaves' produced in a batch is determined by counting them. You might produce 10 loaves, or 11 loaves, or 12 loaves.

You cannot produce exactly 10.73 loaves as a final production count for a batch. The possible outcomes are distinct, separate whole numbers.
Therefore, it is a discrete random variable.

Option C is correct.
