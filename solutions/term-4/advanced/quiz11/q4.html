Problem

The graph of \( y = f(x) \) is shown below.

Which graph could represent \( y = f'(x) \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Link Turning Points to Roots

Where the original function \( f(x) \) has a turning point (local maximum or minimum), its slope is zero. Therefore, its derivative \( f'(x) \) must cross the \(x\)-axis at those exact locations.

Looking at \( f(x) \), there are three turning points:
  • One turning point where \( x < 0 \) (negative).
  • Two turning points where \( x > 0 \) (positive).
This means \( f'(x) \) must have exactly one negative root and two positive roots.

Step 2: Determine Polynomial Degree

Use the overall shape of the graph to determine its polynomial degree and direction.

\( f(x) \) is a 'W' shaped curve that opens upwards. This is the characteristic shape of a positive quartic (degree 4) polynomial.

The derivative of a positive degree 4 polynomial is a positive degree 3 polynomial (a positive cubic).

Step 3: Combine & Select

We are looking for a positive cubic graph (starts bottom-left, ends top-right) with three specific roots.

Option B is the only graph that fits both criteria:

  • It is a positive cubic graph.
  • It crosses the \(x\)-axis exactly once on the negative side and twice on the positive side.

Therefore, Option B is correct.
