Problem

The angle between the line \( y = \frac{2-4x}{3} \) and the positive \( x \)-axis is \( \theta \).

What is the value of \( \theta \), correct to the nearest degree?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Find the Gradient (\(m\)) of the Line

First, rearrange the given equation into gradient-intercept form (\(y = mx + c\)) to identify the slope:

\( y = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{4}{3}x \)
\( y = -\frac{4}{3}x + \frac{2}{3} \)

From this, the gradient is \( m = -\frac{4}{3} \).

Step 2: Relate the Gradient to Trigonometry

The gradient of a straight line is equal to the tangent of its angle of inclination:

\( \tan \theta = m \)

\( \tan \theta = -\frac{4}{3} \)

Step 3: Find the Acute Related Angle

To find the angle easily using the diagram, let's first find the acute related angle by ignoring the negative sign:

\( \text{Related Angle} = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) \approx 53.13^\circ \)

Step 4: Use the Diagram to find \( \theta \)

Since the line has a negative gradient, it slopes downwards, creating an obtuse angle with the positive \( x \)-axis. The related angle represents the acute angle on the other side.

From the straight line (\( 180^\circ \)), we subtract our acute related angle:

\( \theta = 180^\circ - 53.13^\circ \)

\( \theta = 126.87^\circ \)

Rounding to the nearest degree gives \( 127^\circ \).

Therefore, the correct statement is C.
