Problem

What are the solutions of \( 3x^2 + 5x - 1 = 0 \)?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Identify the coefficients

For the standard quadratic equation \( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \), identify the values of \( a \), \( b \) and \( c \) from \( 3x^2 + 5x - 1 = 0 \):

  • \( a = 3 \)
  • \( b = 5 \)
  • \( c = -1 \)

Step 2: Use the reference sheet to find the Quadratic Formula

\( x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \)

Step 3: Substitute the values into the formula

\( x = \frac{-(5) \pm \sqrt{(5)^2 - 4(3)(-1)}}{2(3)} \)

Step 4: Simplify the denominator

Denominator: \( 2(3) = 6 \)

Step 5: Simplify inside the square root (discriminant)

\( (5)^2 - 4(3)(-1) \)

\( 25 - (-12) \)

\( 25 + 12 = 37 \)

Step 6: Combine for final answer

\( x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{37}}{6} \)

Therefore, the correct statement is A.
