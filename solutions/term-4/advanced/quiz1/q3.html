Problem

A standard deck of playing cards is shuffled and two cards are drawn at random.

What is the probability that the two cards are different colours?

Worked Solution

Step 1: Understand Drawing Without Replacement

A standard deck has 52 cards: 26 Red and 26 Black.

When two cards are "drawn at random," we assume they are drawn without replacement (you don't put the first card back). This directly affects the probabilities for the second card.

Crucial concept: Once the first card is taken out, there are only 51 cards left in the deck, so the denominator for the second draw must be 51!

Step 2: Construct a Tree Diagram

Let's map out the possible outcomes and their probabilities using a probability tree.

Step 3: Identify the Favourable Paths

We want the probability that the two cards are different colours. Looking at our tree diagram, there are two paths that satisfy this:

  • Red then Black
  • Black then Red

Step 4: Calculate the Total Probability

Multiply along the branches to find the probability of each path, then add them together (since it is an "OR" situation):

\( P(\text{Different}) = P(\text{Red, Black}) + P(\text{Black, Red}) \)

\( P(\text{Different}) = \left(\frac{26}{52} \times \frac{26}{51}\right) + \left(\frac{26}{52} \times \frac{26}{51}\right) \)

\( P(\text{Different}) = \frac{13}{51} + \frac{13}{51} \)

\( P(\text{Different}) = \frac{26}{51} \)

Therefore, the correct statement is B.
