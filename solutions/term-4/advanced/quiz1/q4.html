Problem

What is the value of the following?

$$ \log_3 \left(\frac{1}{27}\right) + \log_3 \sqrt{3} $$

Worked Solution

Step 1: Recall the relevant Index Laws

Before evaluating the logarithms, we need to rewrite the inner terms using powers of 3.

Negative Index Law

\( \displaystyle \frac{1}{x^n} = x^{-n} \)

Fractional Index Law

\( \displaystyle \sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}} \)

Step 2: Evaluate the first term

Apply the negative index law to rewrite \( \frac{1}{27} \):

\( \displaystyle \frac{1}{27} = \frac{1}{3^3} = 3^{-3} \)

Now substitute this back into the logarithm. Since \( \log_a(a^x) = x \), we get:

\( \log_3(3^{-3}) = -3 \)

Step 3: Evaluate the second term

Apply the fractional index law to rewrite \( \sqrt{3} \):

\( \displaystyle \sqrt{3} = 3^{\frac{1}{2}} \)

Substitute this back into the logarithm:

\( \log_3(3^{\frac{1}{2}}) = \frac{1}{2} \)

Step 4: Add the evaluated terms together

Combine the results from Step 2 and Step 3:

\( \displaystyle -3 + \frac{1}{2} \)

\( \displaystyle -\frac{6}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \)

\( \displaystyle = -\frac{5}{2} \)

Therefore, the correct statement is A.
